OVERVIEW

What is a Trust?

A trust is a legal arrangement where a trustee holds and manages assets on behalf of beneficiaries as per the terms and conditions set by the settlor (the person who creates the trust).

A trust can help you protect your assets, ensure they are used for the right purpose, provide financial security for your family members and offer tax and estate planning benefits.

KEY BENEFITS

Why Create a Trust?

Asset Protection

Keep your assets safe and secure for future generations.

Control & Flexibility

Set clear rules for how your assets are managed and used.

Tax Planning

Potential tax benefits as per applicable laws.

Privacy & Confidentiality

Your financial matters remain private and confidential.

WHO SHOULD CONSIDER A TRUST?

Is a Trust Right for You?

  • Individuals with significant assets or multiple properties
  • Business owners who want to ensure smooth succession
  • Parents who want to provide long-term financial security for their children
  • Individuals who wish to support dependent family members
  • Anyone looking for privacy and better control over asset distribution

A Will only comes into effect after the demise of the testator and often requires court probate. In contrast, a Trust can be created and operational during your lifetime (living trust) or take effect upon demise (testamentary trust), allowing smooth, private asset management and wealth transfer without probate delays.

While trusts are often thought of for large estates, they are equally practical for families with specific goals regardless of net worth—such as securing educational maintenance for minor children, caring for a dependent with special needs, or safeguarding family property from future disputes.

Yes, if you execute a revocable trust, you can modify its terms, add or remove assets, change beneficiaries, or revoke the entire trust at any time during your lifetime. In an irrevocable trust, modifications cannot be made once executed without beneficiary consent or court approval.

Under the Indian Registration Act and the Indian Trusts Act, 1882, registration of a trust deed is mandatory if it involves immovable property.
