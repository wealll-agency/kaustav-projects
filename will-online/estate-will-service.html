FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Common Questions About Estate Will

An Estate Will provides a comprehensive legal framework for distributing your entire wealth, including complex real estate, businesses, investments, and personal liabilities. In contrast, a Family Will focuses primarily on family maintenance, minor guardianship, and personal welfare.

Yes, you can bequeath all self-acquired properties, bank accounts, equity investments, business holdings, vehicles, and valuables. You can also outline the settlement of any debts or liabilities to ensure a smooth transition.

Registration is not mandatory under the Indian Succession Act, 1925. However, registering an Estate Will with the Sub-Registrar provides strong legal validity, minimizes the risk of fraudulent claims, and significantly eases probate.

Yes, appointing an executor is strongly advised for an Estate Will. The executor is legally empowered to administer your estate, settle outstanding obligations, and ensure assets are distributed strictly according to your instructions.

You should review and update your Estate Will whenever significant life changes occur—such as the purchase or sale of properties, business restructuring, changes in beneficiaries, or shifts in tax regulations.
