OVERVIEW

What is a Succession Certificate?

A succession certificate is a legal document issued by a competent court that gives you the authority to collect and manage the financial assets and securities of a deceased person, such as bank accounts, fixed deposits, shares, mutual funds and other investments.

It helps the legal heirs to access the deceased person's financial assets, ensure a smooth transfer and avoid disputes among family members.

Is a Succession Certificate Right for You?

  • Legal heirs of a person who has passed away
  • Individuals with bank accounts, fixed deposits or investments in the name of the deceased
  • Family members who need to collect shares, mutual funds or other securities
  • Those facing delays or difficulties in accessing financial assets
  • Anyone who wants a legally valid document to manage and transfer financial assets

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Common Questions About Succession Certificate

A Will is an estate planning instrument executed by a person during their lifetime specifying asset distribution upon death. A succession certificate is a court-issued legal certificate under the Indian Succession Act, 1925, typically granted when a person passes away intestate (without a valid will), authorizing legal heirs to realize debts, securities, and financial accounts.

Any legal heir of the deceased person—such as the surviving spouse, children, parents, or other Class-I or Class-II legal heirs according to applicable succession laws—can file a petition in the competent civil court for a succession certificate.

A succession certificate covers movable financial assets and debts, including bank account balances, fixed deposits, mutual funds, provident fund (PF) amounts, demat shares, government bonds, and insurance proceeds. It does not apply to immovable properties like land, residential flats, or commercial buildings.

The process typically takes around 5 to 8 months. This timeline accounts for petition filing, mandatory public notices in local and national newspapers, court hearings, and issuance of the certificate by the court after considering objections, if any.
